# Plan: mounts from any angle (horses, other ground mounts, dragons and gryphons) (Sam, 25 Oct)

Not started beyond stage 0. Build each stage only when a film needs it (Sam: save usage for films).
Model for building: Opus, high effort (CLAUDE.md, "building anything new").

## Stage 0 (done, 25 Oct): the side-on horse
`source/mounts.py`: horse, pony, donkey, side-on facing left or right; stand, walk, trot, canter, gallop from
Muybridge's photographs and the real footfall orders; a rider who sits, rises at the trot, and is up off the saddle
at the gallop; reins and stirrups; checks in pre-flight (rule S1). Cost: about one chat.

## How "any angle" works (the same idea as `views.py` for people)
- **One 3D creature, drawn flat.** The skeleton and the motion stay as they are (they are already worked out side-on,
  in metres). The body becomes a row of rounded cross-sections along the spine (like slices of a loaf); each is
  turned to the camera, and the outline of all of them together is inked once. The neck and head are rows of their own,
  so they nod and turn. From the side this gives back today's drawing; from the front, the back or three-quarter it
  gives the right shape without drawing each view by hand.
- **Details live on the surface** (eyes, nostrils, ears, bridle, saddle cloth, mane) and show only on the side facing
  us, so nothing appears through the head from behind.
- **Drawing order by angle.** From the front the neck and chest are drawn over the rider's legs and the hind legs
  behind the body; from behind it is the reverse; the rider always sits on top of the back.
- **Angles:** the same eight directions as people (front, three-quarter, side, three-quarter back, back, each
  mirrored), so horse and rider always agree. Turns pop from one to the next, as the series' turns do.
- **Fluid movement** adds smooth changes of pace (walk to trot to gallop at the end of a stride, so hooves never
  slide), turning while moving, stopping, and set moves (rear, buck, getting on and off).

## Stages and their usage
Usage is estimated against Power Crime Action (about 700 steps for the whole film; a film is roughly a quarter of a
week's usage). One review round (a sheet or clip, your notes, the fixes) adds roughly 1% of a week.

| stage | what you get | size | usage (of a week) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Any-angle horse | horse, pony, donkey from all 8 directions with the rider, every pace; front and back drawing order; checks | one chat, 80-120 steps | about 3-5% |
| 2. Fluid riding | smooth pace changes, turning, stopping, rear, buck, getting on and off | one chat, 60-90 steps | about 2-3% |
| 3. Other ground mounts | each one is a body plan plus its own gait on the same kit: camel (pace gait, hump), elephant (slow amble), big cat or wolf (bounding gallop, bending spine), giant bird or ostrich (two legs) | 40-60 steps each | about 1.5-2% each |
| 4. Flying mounts | dragon and gryphon: wings with three joints (feathered for the gryphon, skin for the dragon); flap (strong downstroke, folded upstroke), glide, hover, take-off and landing; legs tucked in the air; the rider leaning into the climb and dive; sky and clouds sliding past | one or two chats, 150-250 steps | about 5-9% |
| **All of it** | stages 1-4 with two creatures in stage 3 | | **about 15-25%**: roughly one film's worth |

Cheapest useful order: stage 1 (it is the base for everything after it), then whatever the next film asks for.
Stage 4 can be built straight after stage 1 if a film needs a dragon before more horse moves.

## What it will not do
- No realistic rendering: everything stays in the house style (flat colours, ink outlines, soft shading).
- In-between angles (say 20 degrees) are not drawn; the creature snaps to the nearest of the eight directions,
  like people do.
