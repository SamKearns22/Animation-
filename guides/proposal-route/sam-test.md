# The Sam test (the proposal route, step 3)

Sam's own pushes, word for word (15 Nov), each of which made Claude's first answer better. Every proposal is judged
against all of them BEFORE Sam sees it. Eight questions at most: a new push is merged into the closest question,
never added as a ninth. The lines under each push say what it asks in general; they never carry the answer to one
case. Changed only through the route itself (`propose.py lock --verdict NAME`).

"Statement 1" is CLAUDE.md's three aims (Sam, 13 Nov): high quality, fewer notes and faults, a film within 15% of a
week.

1. **Expert.** "Is this what you would suggest as an AI efficiency expert?" Would a top expert in AI agent
   workflows (and in this kind of film-making) propose this? What would they do differently?
2. **Best result.** "What would get us the best result? Checking the cheapest and dearest or the whole chats of our
   last two films? Or just this film" Would a different scope or method get a better result? Is the evidence behind
   it wide enough, and the right evidence for the decision?
3. **Comprehensive.** "Please critique that rule and make sure it will comprehensively deliver" How could a chat
   comply on paper and still deliver a half-effort? Does it find why the problem happens (the root cause, with
   evidence)? How will we know it worked? Will it actually happen in every chat, or does it rely on a chat reading
   something and choosing to follow it?
4. **Most intelligent.** "Is this the most intelligent thing to do and the best way to adhere to statement 1?"
   Were fundamentally different approaches weighed, and is this the best of them?
5. **Risk.** "Saying yes to anything now carries huge risk." What could go wrong if Sam says yes? Can it be undone?
   Is Sam being asked to trust something that has not been shown to work?
6. **Checked.** Is every fact and number in it checked against the record, with its source named, or marked
   "unchecked"?
7. **Not the cheap version.** "The cheapest tools and fixes will always give us the worse results ... Fixes to the
   project are the worst time to save money as they need to deliver huge results." Is it sized by the result it must
   deliver, not by its price? Is there a cheap-but-worse trap in it? (Its honest cost is still stated.)
8. **Nothing temporary.** "I don't like the idea of something becoming temporary." No trials and nothing
   provisional: a change is made properly or not at all, and is reviewed against the film's results when Sam
   declares the film complete.
