# Tone guide: the feel of an A24 horror film

The director's aim: the trailer should evoke the style and quality of an A24 horror production (*The Witch*,
*Hereditary*, *Midsommar*, *Saint Maud*, *Talk to Me*). We borrow the craft, never the brand: no A24 logo,
name, fonts or anything that could pass as theirs. Read this before planning or changing any shot.

## The markers we aim for
1. **Dread over shock.** Unease builds slowly; the audience senses something is wrong before anything
   happens. No cheap jump scares. The one hard shock (the THUD and the cut to black) is earned by the calm
   before it.
2. **Horror inside the ordinary.** A warm kitchen, Christmas, a mother and daughter. The wrongness comes from
   inside the familiar - the horror is how normal her smile is.
3. **Restraint.** Show less. Cut away before the worst moment; leave it to the imagination and the sound.
   Ambiguity is fine: never explain why she does it.
4. **Underplayed performances.** Small, real, human: a genuine smile, a blink, a glance. Nothing theatrical.
   Stillness is powerful (the girl barely moves).
5. **Composed, patient camera.** Mostly still, carefully framed shots - often centred or symmetrical - held
   longer than feels comfortable. Camera moves are slow and motivated (following her gaze), never frantic or
   handheld. Wide shots may leave figures small in the frame.
6. **A restrained, distinctive look.** Our graphite drawing with colour only on food, plants and decorations
   (and later blood) is our signature: muted overall, one or two colours that mean something (the red hat,
   the ham, later the blood).
7. **Sound does half the work.** Silence and near-silence; everyday sounds made too present (the chop, a
   breath); familiar music turned wrong (Deck the Halls sagging); hard cuts from sound to silence and back.
8. **Trailer rhythm.** Long quiet holds early; cutting quickens towards the end; short, sparse title cards on
   black (clean, simple type, few words); an abrupt final cut to black with the sound carrying on.
9. **Something human underneath.** The film is about a family - love, trust, a child watching her mother.
   The horror should land because we care.

## How we check a shot against it
- Does it build unease rather than announce it?
- Is anything shown that would be stronger left unseen?
- Is the acting small enough to be believed?
- Is the camera still or moving slowly for a reason?
- Is the sound doing its share - including silence?
- Would a stranger watching it cold feel uneasy?
