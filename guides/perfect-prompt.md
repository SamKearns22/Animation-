# The "perfect prompt" for Beaver vs Asteroid, and what it teaches

## The prompt

> **Project:** a 45-second comedy animation for TikTok, called "Beaver vs Asteroid".
>
> **The joke:** a completely ordinary, real beaver casually saves the planet from an asteroid
> and does not care at all. The comedy comes from realism and deadpan. The beaver must never
> look or sound like a cartoon, because that gives the joke away. The bigger and more serious
> the threat feels, the funnier its indifference is.
>
> **Look:**
> - Coloured pencil on off-white paper, with visible pencil strokes and paper texture.
> - Backgrounds are simple and minimalist: forest, water, dam, sky.
> - The beaver is drawn realistically, like an illustration in a non-fiction book about animals
>   (see reference photo 1): wet, clumpy brown fur, small black eyes, a leathery nose, orange
>   incisors, dark clawed paws, big webbed back feet, a flat scaly tail. No cartoon eyes or
>   expressions.
> - The beaver holds a small leafy twig in its front paws for the whole film.
>
> **Format:** vertical 9:16 MP4 with sound, 45 seconds. Keep it under 50 MB, and also make a
> copy under 10 MB for Discord. It will mostly be watched on phone speakers.
>
> **Shots and timing:**
> 1. **0–3s, wide forest shot.** The beaver sits on its dam, slowly lifting the twig towards
>    its mouth. Birdsong.
> 2. **3–16s, the threat builds slowly (let it breathe).** A great light grows from above. A deep
>    rumble builds until the ground shakes. First a few leaves fall, then the water starts to
>    splash, then sticks drop off the dam one by one. From 5s the camera very slowly pulls back
>    a little, adding weight, and stops dead at the launch. The beaver just sits there. It
>    glances up at the sky once around 12s, and quickly once more at 15s.
> 3. **16s, launch, without warning.** At the peak of the rumble the beaver rockets straight up
>    on a column of flame and smoke.
> 4. **17–19s, medium distance.** Blue sky with puffy white clouds. The beaver is small but clearly
>    visible. It flies into a big cloud, vanishes, and bursts out of the top.
> 5. **19–22.5s, far away (reference photo 2).** A sunlit sea of clouds seen from high up. The
>    beaver is just a brown dot on a flame, rising on a tall vapour trail.
> 6. **22.5–28.5s, space (reference photos 3 and 4).** The curve of the Earth with a sunrise on
>    the rim. The beaver is a tiny white-hot streak rising out of the atmosphere. Silence and
>    majesty. Then the camera slowly pulls back to reveal a huge burning asteroid heading for
>    Earth.
> 7. **28.5–30.5s, the face (the key comedy beat).** A close-up of the beaver's utterly normal
>    face, surrounded by white fire like a Super Saiyan. Total silence. One slow blink.
> 8. **30.5–33.5s.** The streak hits the asteroid, the beaver smashes through the rock inside,
>    bursts out the other side as a streak of light, and the asteroid explodes into fragments
>    in a flash of white that fills the screen.
> 9. **34.5–45s, back home.** The beaver drops onto its dam with a thud, singed and smoking. It
>    sits a moment, then calmly eats the leaves off its twig as tiny burning rocks rain down on
>    the forest. One lands on its head. It keeps chewing.
>
> **Life details:** in the forest scenes, occasional nose twitches and blinks.
>
> **Sound (realistic and understated, no cartoon noises):**
> - The rumble should feel planet-shaking, including on a phone speaker.
> - Launch: a blast, then a roar.
> - Space: near silence, then a deep threatening rumble as the asteroid appears.
> - The face: complete silence, with no click where the sound cuts out.
> - Asteroid: exploding rock, then a split second of silence before a huge explosion.
> - Landing: a single dull thud.
> - Ending: small sizzles and quiet chewing.
>
> **Process:** before animating, send me 3–4 still images to approve the look. Then send a full
> draft with sound. Explain your choices and your limitations.

## Lessons for next time

1. **Explain the joke, and what would ruin it.** "Keep it deadpan and realistic, no cartoon
   noises" is more useful than any single visual detail. When the AI knows *why*, it makes
   hundreds of small decisions the right way.
2. **Attach reference pictures at the start.** Your four photos changed the film more than
   anything else. One picture of the style you want saves several rounds of back-and-forth.
3. **Say the camera distance for each moment.** Words like *wide shot*, *medium shot*,
   *close-up* and *pull back* turn a list of events into a film. See the glossary below.
4. **Give seconds, not just order.** Say how long the whole film is and how long each moment
   lasts, and name the moments to linger on. Build-ups almost always need more time than you
   first think; ours grew from 3 to 13 seconds.
5. **Direct the sound as carefully as the picture.** Describe each sound and each silence.
   Silence is a tool: the silent close-up is the biggest laugh.
6. **Add acting notes.** Blinks, nose twitches and glances are what make a character feel alive.
7. **Say where it will be watched.** Phone speakers, TikTok and Discord each changed technical
   choices (bass, file size, shape).
8. **Ask for stills first.** Approving the look before the full animation is cheap; changing it
   afterwards means redoing everything.
9. **Expect a few rounds.** Even the perfect prompt gets a strong first draft, not a finished
   film. Watching it is how you discover what it needs.
10. **Keep giving feedback like you did:** specific, with the reason ("cartooning reduces the
    absurdity"). That was the single most useful thing you did in this project.

## Glossary

- **Beat:** one moment or event in the story.
- **Wide shot:** far away, showing the whole scene. **Medium shot:** the character plus some
  surroundings. **Close-up:** mostly the face.
- **Pull back / zoom out:** the camera moves away, revealing more.
- **Cut:** an instant switch from one shot to another.
- **Hold / linger:** stay on a moment longer so it sinks in.
- **Pacing:** how fast or slow the story moves.
- **Deadpan:** comedy delivered with a completely straight face.
- **Hook:** whatever grabs the viewer in the first 1–2 seconds.
