# The rulebook: every rule once, with the check that holds it

**Contents:** Planning (1-86) · Building (2-83, S, L) · Checks (5-85) · Notes, review and sound (29-88, S) ·
Working with Sam (the CLAUDE.md rules, by section) · Merged numbers (where an old number now lives).

Every film follows this page (Yuletide is a separate experiment with its own guides). Each rule is written here once; other guides say *how* (the tools,
the method) and point here by number. **A rule only holds if a check enforces it:** "check" names the tool that
pre-flight runs, or "by hand" (ticked on the check sheet before every animatic, or searched for at the handover).
When Sam approves a rule, it gets a row here in the same chat, with the next free number (the next is 90). Numbers
are never reused or changed: a merged rule's number is listed at the end with the rule that now holds it. Why each
rule exists (the film and note that taught it) is in git's history and `story/archive.md`, not here.

Working-with-Sam rules (asking, approvals, messages, handovers, models) live in `CLAUDE.md` only; they are listed at
the end by section.

## Planning
| # | Rule | Check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One plan of the world in metres per film: every place, opening, camera and facing; every shot reads from it. The layout is locked in planning (who sits or stands where, gaps, what must be in frame); a later change is named as a cost. | `plan.py` (pre-flight, with its true-gap check; skipped for a film with no rooms: `HAS`); the layout by hand |
| 3 | Before building any place: 8-10 things that make it recognisable, our picture beside Sam's references on one sheet, each ticked. No references: stop and ask; never draw from memory. Before every animatic, any real place in the film (a landmark, a junction, a building) is shown on the check sheet beside Sam's reference photo or video frame and compared point by point; anything moving through it (cars, people) stays where the real thing allows (on the road, on the path) (Elder Scrolls: Swindon, 9 Nov). | `benchmark.py` (pre-flight); the film's own route check (e.g. `traffic_faults`: every car on the road); critique S3, S2 |
| 8 | Rehearse the 3-4 riskiest moves as 2-3 s clips before building the rest; anything that falls or piles up always counts (takes in old 40). Showstopper Shots (Sam names them): the elements the screenplay implies (terrain, vehicles, props, how each moves, the action round it, effects) listed for Sam to approve or trim, ticked at the first clip. | `preflight.gate_warnings`: no "Rehearsal clips sent:" line (date and file, or "none: why") refuses a film started from the 10 Nov template, warns on older ones |
| 10 | Framing and timing planned up front (takes in old 25): camera distance, who looks where, and each key beat's hold (about 0.3 s per readable stage); no diagonal cameras unless the models hold up. (The Hook Text's place: rule 75.) | `LOOKS_AT` eyelines; holds: critique M2 |
| 11 | One rough composition sheet (the 3-4 key shots) before building. It is sent once: notes on it are applied, never re-sent for approval. | `preflight.gates` ("Composition sheet sent:" in the notes); by hand |
| 14 | Pre-flight refuses to run while the brief checklist has a blank "done?" row or no composition sheet was sent; a shot missing anyone in `SHOT_CAST` fails. | `preflight.gates`, `cast_faults` |
| 24 | The estimate and the forecast (Sam, 11 Nov; merges old 24 and 44, which took in old 36, 39, 54). Budget by the film's size (Sam, 11 Nov, after the budget trials): about 15% of a week for 3 new characters and one place, about 26% for 9 and 3; always aim lower. Before Sam says go: every thing in the brief marked KNOWN (catalogue, pose gallery, Passed the critique: almost free) or NEW; each new build and risky move with its own cost, why it is 3D (only when the camera moves round it, 3+ angles must agree, or it turns in the hand; otherwise one flat painting per camera), which parts the camera sees (build only those), and any slow tool. Every new character and place gets a tier: Lead (speaks or has a close-up), Support, or Glimpse (on screen under about 4 seconds, or never taller than a third of the frame); a Glimpse gets one view and one pose. Sam gives a picture and up to three must-haves for each new character and names the 2-4 showstopper moments; anything added after go is priced before it is built. Each line's cost comes from the nearest measured past cost in the ledgers, naming it; a line with nothing measured behind it is marked 'unmeasured', and the estimate's first line says how much of the total is unmeasured. Starting units (the 11 Nov trials, critique helpers counted; a film's own ledger replaces them): a Lead 3.2, a frame of up to five Glimpses 3.7, a critique helper 0.5, a notes round 2.7, a Sonnet finishing chat 0.35. Notes rounds are priced from past films' real rounds and notes, not hoped for. No padding, no multipliers: one honest number per line. The plan is split into stages (cast, sets, moves, animatic 1, each notes round, final), each with its share. The moves' share cannot be spent by the cast or the sets: rehearsal clips of the showstoppers are made before detail, and animatic 1 always carries the acting list (never 'holds and cuts'). At the end of every stage the first line to Sam is the forecast from the real spend: 'Spent X% of the Y% planned so far; at this rate the film lands at Z%.' When a stage reaches its share, or the forecast passes the estimate, work stops for Sam's Y with what exists, what's left and the cheapest way on that keeps the quality. Quality is never cut to fit. Something built for one film only stays in its estimate. After posting, `audit` compares each estimate line with its real cost, so the next estimate starts from better numbers. | `ledger.py estimate FILM PCT --stages` and `ledger.py stage FILM NAME` (the forecast line); `ledger.py auto` (the Stop hook: every chat's cost after every reply; stops the chat when the stage being worked on reaches its share and at each half share beyond, at the film's estimate and every quarter over, and when the forecast passes by a quarter); kittests `estimate_forecast_and_alarm`, `stage_share_stop`; the template's estimate table |
| 26 | Sound at planning (takes in old 27): every sound effect listed and asked for in one message, placeholders named in every send. Bleeps: Sam always says the word and I bleep over it (never ask), placed from the sound; every swear is bleeped, asterisks or not (Sam, 4 Nov). | `voices.bleep_check` (pre-flight; kittest `bleeps_every_swear`); where the bleep sits: by the sound |
| 28 | Reuse first (`guides/kit-catalogue.md`); every new build is added to the catalogue when building ends. | `catalogue.py` sheet; updating by hand |
| 38 | Front on by default (each person within about 25 degrees of the lens). A three-quarter view is a last resort (Sam, 3 Nov): only when no other way makes the shot work, listed with that reason. Characters are built in 2D first: front, side and back; a three-quarter view (or three-quarter back) is built only when the script calls for one, and named in the plan with the shot that needs it (Sam, 4 Nov). | `plan.py` (`SHOT_MARKS`, `THREE_QUARTER_OK`); the cast sheet shows front, side, back only |
| 74 | Before the first animatic, every line gets an acting plan in the film's notes (shot and why, face, body, the word the move lands on), shown to Sam with the brief questions. Before the list is written, ask Sam what each line means wherever the tone could go two ways (sincere or wink, hopeful or doubtful), my pick first; there is no limit on questions, because a question costs far less than a fix (Sam, 7 Nov). Every talking character's head acts on the voice (tilts and nods from `headlife`, never a still talking photo), custom-drawn creatures included (Sam, 11 Nov). | `preflight.gate_warnings`: no acting list refuses a film started from the 10 Nov template, warns on older ones; ticked on the check sheet; layout in `guides/satire.md`, "The acting list"; `preflight.head_life_faults`: a speaker with no `HEAD_LIFE` or a still head through a line |
| 86 | Tools (anything built for every film: a check, a kit, a helper) get their own chat and their own estimate, outside any film's 12%. The estimate comes first, and building starts only on Sam's yes. If a film needs a new tool, the film's chat stops and asks. Something built for one film only stays in that film's estimate (rule 24) (Sam, 9 Nov). | by hand (the tool's estimate and Sam's yes in its notes) |

| 13 | Every check lives in the shared kit and runs on every film; never copy a check from a film file. | `kitchecks.py`; moving figures against the set: `setcheck.moving_faults` (pre-flight, any film with `SET_FEET`; one with people and `SET_BOXES` but no `SET_FEET` is named) |
| 20 | Held things aim in 3D with the holder's body, never at a fixed screen angle. | `viewchecks.turn_faults` |
| 22 | My set dressing is shown on the cast or composition sheet, clear of every action spot, and in the audit. | `audit.py`; critique S1 |
| 32 | Falling pieces settle broadest side down, never overlap or rest on another piece, shadow only where they touch; long pieces land flat, never on their end; a saved drawing records its camera. | `lying.py contact_faults` (bodies); pieces by hand (tilt at landing) |
| 41 | Bodies and hands (Sam, 4-10 Nov; merges old 41, 42, 81, and 21 and 64 (Job J)). Poses come from the tested gallery (`guides/pose-gallery.md`) and are never shown from a view its row says to avoid; a new pose is tested from every view first (standing 6, seated 5, lying 12) and its build priced at planning. Every arm ends in a visible hand, every frame, every view; a point shows one finger out along the forearm. Arms and body are one piece: one outline from the collar over the shoulder and down the arm, no line, cap, notch or step at the join (an armpit crease only when the arm is lifted). A standing person never bobs: breathing is a lean. A limb pointing at the lens is foreshortened and drawn over the body. Standing at rest the legs are straight and the elbows bend a touch outward (`references/figure-rig/`). Lying, a neck shows, a limp arm lies flat, and each arm stays on its own side. Every cast sheet draws the arms exactly as the film does. | `handcheck.py` every frame (hands, points, hands meeting props, props hidden, the shoulder seam); `posecheck.py` in pre-flight (avoid views, poses with no gallery row, lying bodies); `posegallery.py`, `sitting.py audit`, `lying.py check`; `kittests.py` (gallery, point, legs and elbows, seam, held props, lying); critique P3, P5, P6, P8 |
| 49 | Every famous head passes the likeness check (60%+, nothing pushed the wrong way, 1st in the line-up) before the cast sheet. It is built from the likeness pack: 3+ straight-on, close, not-smiling photos (plus three-quarter, talking, caricatures); photos beat caricatures. | `likeness.py check`; `likeness.py card` warns on a thin pack |
| 50 | Every famous person gets one shot with the face at least 240 px tall (of 1920). | `kitchecks.faces` from the film's `FAMOUS` list (pre-flight; kittest `famous_face_size`) |
| 63 | Running straight at the lens uses the kit's `front_run` gait and `front_run_arms` (low knees, tucked elbows, fists pumping forward at the chest and belt), never the side-on run or arms out to the side. | `moves.audit` (gait numbers); `handcheck`; critique M3 |
| 65 | A speaker with no face of its own (the Sorting Hat) opens its mouth with the voice's loudness, one syllable at a time. | `mouths.loudness_open`; `voices.py` mouth check |
| 77 | Anyone with facial hair: run the beard tool first; Sam confirms its five plain lines (moustache, patch under the lip, chin, cheeks, colour), correcting any in a word, before the cast sheet (Sam, 7 Nov). | `beardkit.py analyse` (its five lines, sent with the empty-face sheet); by hand |
| 78 | Anyone resting on a surface (desk, table, wall, rail) shows the weight: the elbow bends slightly, the forearm comes forward and turns a little, and no part of the arm, hand or body goes below the surface; a limb reaching out ends in a clear shape, never half hidden inside something (Sam, 7 Nov). | critique P1, P7 (full size, beside the reference photo (`references/body-weight/leaning-on-table-forearm-forward.jpg`: head on one fist, other forearm forward and turned on the table, elbow bent)); and `kitchecks` (rests, 8 Nov): every arm the film's notes list as resting goes in the film's `RESTS = [(forearm, surface, from, to, why)]`; pre-flight flags a forearm or elbow more than 1 cm below the surface, an elbow floating over it or off its edge, a listed arm it cannot find (COULD NOT TEST), and an arm the sitting kit poses resting that `RESTS` leaves out |
| 80 | Anyone seated front on shows knees and lap, and rising or sitting never makes the head dip below its seated height (Sam, 7 Nov; Islamocommunism note 10). | `sitting.py` checks (`check_no_sink`, the lap drawn when `j['lap']`); critique P7 |
| 83 | In a flat painted set, no painted object overlaps another unless the set lists it as meant (Sam, 8 Nov, Ad-Vanced Notice note 42b: the portrait clipped into the mantel clock). Each set lists its objects' boxes (`SET_BOXES`) and its meant overlaps with why (`SET_MEANT`). | `setcheck.py` (the film's checks(), so pre-flight) |
| S2 | A hand moves the object, drawn over it; a resting hand keeps 3 cm clear. | `kitchecks` (`TOUCHES`, `FREE_MOVES`) |
| S5 | On every character, the pupil and iris stay inside the eye's outline at any size or glance (Sam, 11 Nov: every cast, not only the stock people). | every cast's eyes cut to the outline (`burnham.face`, `remakes_cast._eye`, new casts `eyekit`); pre-flight's house style names a cast file that draws uncut pupils (`housestyle.eye_faults`); critique P14 |
| S7 | No off-style shorthand (stars, sweat drops, motion lines); effect shapes are checked: no star, regular spike or motion line. | `viewchecks.shape_faults` (`EFFECTS`); critique O6 |
| S8 | Bullet holes and marks only on real surfaces. | `kitchecks.marks` |
| S10 | Riding: `mounts.py`, real pace speeds, the rider's seat matches the pace. | `mounts.check` |
| L1 | People lying down or seen from high have real depth (one 3D body, one outline, resting on the floor). | `lying.py`; critique P7 |

## Checks (what pre-flight and the tools must do)
| # | Rule | Check |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | One automatic collision test on every frame, swept areas included; only listed contacts allowed. | 6 | One automatic pop-in test: anything appearing, vanishing or jumping away from the frame edge is flagged. Every group (crowd, police) arrives, stops and leaves on screen, never popping (old 9). | `audit.py` (`ALLOWED_POPS`); critique M1 |
| 7 | Script and mouth audit: recordings against script and subtitles; a mouth opens once a syllable, shut in silence. | `voices.py --film` |
| 16 | Direction holds across a cut unless the plan says it swaps. | `viewchecks.direction_faults` (`movers(t)`) |
| 17 | Every figure in front of an effect hides it. | `viewchecks.hide_faults` |
| 18 | Every joke prop at least 40 px (of 1920) at phone size. | `viewchecks.readability_faults` (`JOKE_PROPS`) |
| 19 | Checks print a short summary; a file is written only when the list is longer than the summary shows. Long check printouts are filtered so only the faults are read. | `report.py` |
| 57 | Nothing flips from in front to behind (or back) between two frames of a shot unless planned (`ORDER_SWAPS`). | `clipcheck.flip_faults` on the film's `draw_order(t)` |
| 58 | Pre-flight runs only the checks for what the film contains (`HAS`: people, rooms, voices, effects, props); each skipped check is named in one line with why. | `preflight.py` |
| 59 | No animatic or final render unless pre-flight passed on the code as it is now; faults Sam has been told about need `--faults-named`, and each is listed once: the film keeps a named-faults list and pre-flight shows only faults not on it. Pre-flight is incremental (Sam, 3 Nov): after a clean run it re-checks only the frames whose picture changed (and a few round them) and the bodies in their shots, and runs the text and sound checks in full; it runs in full when there is no earlier clean run, the last run found a frame-level fault, the check code or the film's allow-lists changed, or with `--full`. | `film_engine.render` (the lock); `preflight.py` (`-frames.json` fingerprints); `kittests.py incremental_preflight_fingerprints`; the named-faults list by hand (the handover's "faults named" line) until pre-flight reads it |
| 62 | Captions are on in every animatic and final, in the series style (set on Remakes, Sam 11 Nov): large white words in Montserrat Black (Gotham Black's free stand-in) with a black outline and shadow, in the lower third, at most 5 words a piece and two rows. Each piece comes up on the syllable it starts with in Sam's take (within 0.3 s of its first word), stays on screen at least 0.8 s, at most 220 words a minute, and is never joined into longer pieces. A wording change never moves the timing: timing always comes from the script as spoken; the changed wording is swapped in afterwards. | `subtitles.py`; pre-flight caption check; `kittests.py subtitles_readable`; the 0.3 s start: `voices.caption_start_check`; the style and timing: `subtitles.draw` and `subtitles.pieces` / `caption_at` (the template uses them); `finish.py` refuses a film with no `caption_at`; `kittests.py finish_captions_by_default` |
| 66 | A render that is refused, stopped or fails leaves no file: the old output is deleted before anything else, so an old render can never be taken for the new one. | `film_engine.render`; `kittests.py render_leaves_no_old_file` |
| 67 | One full pre-flight per round: make every fix, run the quick check (`preflight.py NAME OUT --text`, seconds) until it is clean, then the full pre-flight once, then the render in the same background chain. A background job is stopped with its own task stop, never by name. | `preflight.py --text`; by hand |
| 70 | Captions, the Hook Text and the sign-off are added at the finishing step (`finish.py`), never in the picture render, so a note on them costs seconds, not a render. Every animatic and final, for every film, is then delivered at 1.1x with voices at their normal pitch (Sam, 11 Nov), after any trimming; the sign-off keeps its 1.6 s (rule 52) and the Hook Text its 0.5 s pop (rule 75) on the delivered file, and the caption and Hook Text checks run at that speed. | `finish.py --film NAME`: captions, then 1.1x with pitch kept (`subtitles.SPEED`), then the Hook Text and sign-off; the caption check (`subtitles.readable_faults`) and Hook Text check run at 1.1x; `kittests.py finish_delivers_at_one_point_one`, `caption_check_at_delivered_speed` |
| 72 | No pose returns to rest in the last 0.4 s before a cut (cut before the return); no hand or arm passes behind the person's own wings, cape or body. A turn between views jumps straight to the other drawing (South Park; the series' standard snap since Ad-Vanced Notice 41b); never a squeezed or stretched 2D swivel (Sam, 11 Nov, Remakes note 67). | the return: `kitchecks.cut_rests` (pre-flight, standing people; kittest `cut_before_the_return`); behind wings or cape: by hand; the turn: critique M3 |
| 75 | The Hook Text is placed when the first animatic is made (Sam, 11 Nov), where it covers nothing important (a face, the action, a sign or prop the joke needs) for as long as it is on screen; its position is the film's `HOOK`, checked on every animatic and final. It pops in 0.5 s into the film, not on the first frame: nothing for the first half second, then a quick pop (small, a touch past full size, settling, about 0.2 s), held through the opening shot. | `finish.py CLEAN OUT --film NAME`: reads `HOOK` and runs `hookcheck.py` first (refuses on a fault unless `--faults-named`; a film with `HOOK` but no `KEEP_CLEAR` is refused); pre-flight's gate stops an animatic until `HOOK` and `KEEP_CLEAR` are written (`hookcheck.plan_faults`); `hook_text.py` (starts at 0.5 s) |
| 85 | Cost watch (Sam, 9 Nov): every chat is measured after each step, its critique helpers' cost included (Sam, 11 Nov: they were a quarter of every build, unseen) and Fable priced at twice Opus for new and written words. Amber at 200k tokens of history or 3% of the week; red at 300k or 5%. Amber: finish the step, then hand over. Red: hand over now (unless Sam types 'override'). Every reply on amber or red opens with a 'Cost:' line. | automatic (`.claude/hooks/costwatch.py`, every step and every message); and per film (Sam, 11 Nov: Remakes ran to 45% on a 14.5% estimate unseen): `ledger.py auto` (the Stop hook) writes every chat's cost into its film's ledger after each reply and pushes it; at the estimate and every quarter over, it stops the chat to tell Sam and ask Y/N to go on |
