# TikTok posting time and strategy: what the research says (7 Oct 2026)

Researched for the CranbriJoos account (deadpan comedy and satire, UK audience, about 29 followers). Sources are
ranked by how much to trust them. **Nothing here is a guarantee: the evidence on posting time is weak, mixed, and
mostly from marketing companies.**

## 1. What TikTok itself says (most trustworthy)
Source: TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou", and the Community Guidelines "For You feed
eligibility standards" (UK page, effective 24 Sep 2026).
- Recommendations rank videos by **user interactions** (likes, shares, follows, comments, and whether someone finishes
  watching), **video information** (captions, sounds, hashtags) and, with lower weight, device and account settings.
- A strong signal, "such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end", counts for more
  than a weak one.
- **Follower count and past hits are not direct factors.** Bigger accounts get more views only because they have more
  followers to show it to.
- **Posting time is not listed as a factor.** (It is not denied either: timing could still help indirectly, through
  who is online when a video first goes out.)
- Very new videos, videos under review and spam may not be recommended. A video not reaching the For You feed does
  not mean it broke a rule; the creator's analytics show if it was made ineligible.
- The For You feed deliberately mixes content and won't show the same sound or creator twice in a row.
- Political content is allowed organically if it follows the guidelines. Paid political ads are banned, and some
  misinformation is ineligible for the For You feed. **No satire-specific rules or data were found.**

## 2. Posting time: the data (mixed, vendor-produced)
| Source | Size | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Buffer (7.1M posts, Sep 2026) | posts scheduled through Buffer; median engagement rate; time zones normalised | Best single slot **Sunday 9am**, then Monday 1pm, Sunday 1pm. Best day Saturday. Evenings 6-11pm strong; afternoons weakest. Says timing is "a boost, not a fix" and that quality matters most. |
| Sprout Social (2026) | about 2 billion engagements, 307,000 profiles, mostly brands | **Tue-Thu 2-6pm best; weekends worst; Sunday worst on every network.** The opposite of Buffer. |
| Hopper HQ (UK) | method not stated | UK: weekdays 7-9am, 12-2pm, 7-10pm; Friday best; Sunday 11am-1pm. |
| Comedy/entertainment vendors (Viraly, Opus) | method not stated | Evenings 8-11pm and weekend afternoons; one says 6-9pm UTC. |
| University student analysis (28,844 posts, Feb 2026) | observational | Hour of day matters a little more than day of week, but "overall differences are modest"; posting time is "a secondary optimisation factor". |

How to read this:
- **The sources disagree**, because each measures a different group (brands vs creators), a different thing
  (engagement rate vs views) and in different ways.
- **The "Sunday 9am golden hour" claim mostly traces back to Buffer's one dataset.** Other blogs repeat it, so they
  are not independent confirmation.
- Buffer's own caveat: "not all studies agree" and results vary by audience and method.
- **With about 29 followers, "when your followers are active" is a very small sample.** A video's first viewers are
  mostly strangers, so general UK habits matter more than your follower graph for now.
- Claims about a "first 30-60 minutes" test with 200-500 viewers, or a follower-only test first, come from third-party
  blogs, not TikTok. They are plausible but unconfirmed.

## 3. What matters more than time (agreement across sources)
- **Watching behaviour comes first:** completion, rewatches, shares, then comments, then likes. A short video watched
  to the end beats a long one that is not (TikTok's own wording, plus every vendor).
- **The opening must give a reason to stay** (a person, a situation, a question). The "3-second rule" is a rule of
  thumb, not an official number; one source warns against treating it as universal.
- **Frequency** (Buffer, 11.4 million posts, same accounts compared with themselves): going from 1 to 2-5 posts a week
  gives up to 17% more views per post; 6-10 gives 29%; 11+ gives 34%. But the **typical post stays about 500 views**;
  only the best 10% rise (about 3,700 views at 1 post a week, 14,400 at 11+). In plain English: **posting more raises
  the ceiling, not the floor. It's more lottery tickets.**
- **Search matters more now:** caption words and spoken words that match what people search for (Sprout, Darkroom).
  Hashtags help with grouping but are a weaker signal.
- **Don't delete underperforming videos** (Sprout): it removes data and heavy deleting may trigger spam filters.
  Setting a video to private keeps its history.
- **Re-using a video:** advice is mixed and mostly from low-quality sources. TikTok says spam and artificial traffic
  may be ineligible. A re-cut with a different opening is clearly a different video.

## 4. Comedy and satire specifically
- **No credible research was found on satire timing or reach.** What exists is vendor advice that entertainment and
  comedy do better in the evening and at weekends ("people open TikTok to unwind"), which is plausible but unproven.
- Comedy-specific creator advice (weak evidence): consistent characters and ongoing storylines build a returning
  audience. Your series approach fits this.
- Topical satire is time-sensitive (about 24-48 hours after the news). No data was found, but the logic is that the
  news cycle matters more than the clock.
- If a satire video seems stuck, check TikTok Studio for "not eligible for recommendation" before assuming the
  audience didn't like it.

## 5. The account's own numbers (small and confounded)
| Video | Posted | Result |
|---|---|---|
| MOSSAD (30 s) | Sun 4 Oct, 9:02am | 6,906 views at 6 h; 17.1 s average watch; 37% to the end |
| The Patriots 2 (60 s) | Mon 5 Oct, 1pm | 255 views at 1.5 h; 14.8 s average; 14% to the end |
| DLC (49 s) | Tue 6 Oct, 5:35pm | 175 views at 2.5 h; 7.3 s average; 3.8% to the end |
These differ in topic, length, opening and age, so **time of day cannot be separated from everything else.** The
clearest pattern is the one TikTok describes: shorter, with a person and a situation from the first frame, was watched
more of the way through.

## 6. Recommended approach
1. Treat timing as a small tie-breaker. **Sunday 9am is a reasonable choice** (one large dataset plus the account's best
   result), but it is not proven.
2. **Test, don't assume:** vary the slot (e.g. Sun 9am, Sat 5pm, Fri 6pm, a weekday 8pm) across the next 8-10 videos and log
   the results in the table above. Expect noise: one hit can swamp any timing effect.
3. Judge each video by **average watch time and the percentage who finish it** in TikTok Studio, not just views.
4. Aim for **2-5 posts a week** while keeping quality; consistent days and times help the audience form a habit.
5. Keep lengths short for topical satire (the Mossad length of about 30 s worked best so far).
6. Put the key search words in the caption and the spoken script.
