# Growth analysis, 3 Nov 2026 (first five weeks)

Data: the 3 Nov snapshot in `story/metrics.md`. Chart: `review/account-5-weeks.png`. Earlier reading:
`story/growth-analysis-18-oct.md` (outside benchmarks, targets).

## Findings
- 26 films in 35 days, 92K views. 8 films with 4,500+ views make 83% of all views: a hit-driven account, as TikTok is.
- Median views per week: 1,352 / 694 / 1,122 / 1,421 / 5,907. The last week (Patriots 3, Patriots 4, TERFwarts) is
  the first where the typical film is a hit. Before 15 Oct: 2 hits in 14 films; since: 6 in 12.
- Reactions: (likes + comments + shares + saves) / views = 8.8% overall, Patriots series 12.2%. Socialinsider 2026
  (brand accounts, 1-5K followers): 4.4%. Hootsuite calls 4%+ exceptional.
- Followers per 1,000 views about 1.8 (promotion excluded); target was 3. Weakest number. Big one-off topical hits
  convert worst (Roadster 0.2, Patriots 4 0.2, TERFwarts 1.1); recurring/character pieces convert best (Patriots 3 3.0,
  Prepper Minister 3.9, Netan-Yahoo! 3.1).
- Patriots series: median 5,311 views vs 1,012 for the rest. The only proven franchise.
- Hits share: a named current UK story or figure in the Hook Text, a laugh in the first 3 s, average watch 16 s+.
  Flops share: niche premise or late first laugh (Strictly Crabs, Flying Whales, Halo No-No, Dam, L&L, DLC).
- Retention as a share of length is below comedy targets (hits 29-55% vs about 55% for 30-60 s comedy).

## Follower growth: what the outside data says (searched 3 Nov)
- 107 (18 Oct) -> 177 (3 Nov): +65% in 16 days, about 25% a week; real followers (minus about 30 promo) 77 -> about 147.
- Published benchmarks are for brand accounts: Swydo "follower growth 10%+ = rapid"; Socialinsider: 1-5K accounts grew
  150% across 2025; Umbrex: about 4% a week. We are far above all of them, but growth from a tiny base is easy.
- No published study of followers per 1,000 views. One public anecdote (Reddit r/GrowthHacking, 1-month experiment,
  1-3 videos a day): 4.4M views -> 10.2K followers, about 2.3 per 1,000 views. Ours is about 1.8: close to normal. So the
  3-per-1,000 target was my guess and too harsh; views, not conversion, are the main lever.
