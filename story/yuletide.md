# Yuletide: the film's premise (director's decision)

**Title:** *Yuletide*.

**The hidden origin of the horror:** the film is built on the "Native American burial ground" idea. It is about
the vengeful spirits of a Native people who were betrayed and slain on Christmas by Jamestown settlers, taking
revenge on a family who now live on the land where they died.

**Inspired by history:** some details of the massacre are inspired by the first known Christmas in what is now
the United States (1539): Hernando de Soto's Spanish expedition held its Christmas Mass in Anhaica, the
Apalachee capital (today's Tallahassee, Florida), which they had seized - taking the people's homes and winter
food - while the Apalachee fought to take it back. The film's massacre itself is set among the Jamestown
settlers (Virginia, from 1607).

**Kept hidden:** for most of the film - and never in the trailer - it is not clear that this is the origin of
the horror. The audience sees only a loving family at Christmas and inexplicable acts of self-harm (the
mother's smile, the cleaver, her own fingers).

## Notes for building it
- **Where the house stands:** the family must live on the land where the massacre happened, so the house is
  in Virginia's Tidewater, near Jamestown - modelled on the Home Alone house (a grand brick Georgian-style
  home, which suits Virginia well) but not in Illinois.
- **Doing the trope well:** the "burial ground" idea is often criticised for turning Native people into
  faceless monsters. An A24-quality treatment makes the betrayal by the settlers the original evil, and treats
  the dead as people wronged rather than creatures. The real peoples of the Jamestown area (the Powhatan
  paramount chiefdom and its member tribes) are still here today; any specific details should be researched
  carefully and respectfully.
- **Clues without telling:** small, easily missed hints can be planted early (a detail in the kitchen, a
  sound under the carol, a date) so the reveal feels inevitable on a second viewing.

## Title cards (director's decision)
Three cards appear during the trailer, at points still to be decided:

> THE FIRST GIFT
> EVER GIVEN
> WAS BLOOD

- Each line is its own card, so the sentence builds across the trailer, the last card landing late.
- Style (see guides/tone.md): plain, clean capitals on black, sparse and quiet - no effects - held just long
  enough to read. The words stay ambiguous: they hint at a sacrifice without revealing the backstory.
