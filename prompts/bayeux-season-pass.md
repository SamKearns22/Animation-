## The joke
A short advertisement for **the Bayeux Tapestry Season Pass**, copying the season passes sold for video games
(extra content for a product you already own). The main product is a precious 950-year-old historical artefact,
so each piece of "extra content" is more absurd than the last.
- The style is a flashy video game trailer, like the season pass trailers for online multiplayer games such as
  Fortnite, Call of Duty and Arc Raiders: hard cuts, big bold text slamming onto the screen, whooshes, bass
  hits, lens flares, glitch transitions, a deep trailer voice.
- **The contrast is the joke:** everything *in* the tapestry stays in the tapestry's own hand-embroidered style
  (the new weapons too), while the trailer effects play over the top with total commitment.
- Play it straight, as a real advert would. No winks.
- **What would ruin it:** the new weapons drawn in a modern cartoon or 3D style instead of embroidery; real game
  logos, fonts or music (copy the feel, never the brand); damaging or mocking the real tapestry itself.

## Art style
- **The tapestry close-ups: copy the Bayeux Tapestry's style as closely as possible.** It is wool embroidery
  on bleached linen:
  - outlines in stem stitch; areas filled with laid-and-couched work (long parallel stitches held down by
    cross stitches), so every filled shape has visible stitch direction and texture;
  - a small palette of plant-dyed wools: terracotta red, ochre yellow, buff, sage and blue-green, dark navy
    blue, grey-black, on an off-white linen ground with its weave visible;
  - flat figures in profile, no shading or perspective; horses and people in non-natural colours (a blue
    horse, a green-legged man);
  - a narrow upper and lower border of birds, beasts and diagonal bars; Latin captions stitched in capitals
    above the action.
  Check every frame against my reference images.
- **The room shots:** our usual satire style (the flat cartoon look of the TikTok series), matching my Tapestry
  Room Reference for the layout and camera.
- Redraw the tapestry in code from the references (it is a public-domain artwork). Never paste a photograph
  into the film.
