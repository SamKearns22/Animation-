# THE PERFECT BRIEF: how to write a film brief, with Cry Minister as the worked example

How to use it: work down the headings in order, like writing a screenplay (idea, world, cast, scenes, sound,
continuity). Under each heading, "FOR ANY FILM" says what to write; "CRY MINISTER" shows it done properly, using what
the 48 notes taught us. Copy the language and level of detail, not the content. Things already fixed in CLAUDE.md and
the guides (TikTok size, sign-off, circle hands, smooth movement, animatic first, asking before renders, no
code-made sound effects) are never repeated.

---

## 1. THE IDEA (the joke in under 60 words)

FOR ANY FILM: who the main character is, what they are doing, what absurd thing happens around them, and what they
refuse to acknowledge. Name the turn (the shot where normal becomes absurd) and the last beat.

CRY MINISTER:
Andy Burnham, the "TikTok Prime Minister", films a sincere address about wolf attacks on British high streets. A
pack of wolves eats a man behind him in the first minute and he never mentions it. He talks about "smaller wolves"
and "means-tested wolfproof armour for pensioners", begs viewers to get a job, falls apart about having no money,
and finishes by running from the wolves while still filming himself selfie-style. The turn is shot 2. The last beat
is the lead wolf leaping at the lens, then a hard cut to black.

## 2. TONE AND LIMITS

FOR ANY FILM: shock level 1-5, what violence or content is allowed and how it is shown, what must never be drawn
(real logos, real accounts, anything that could read as rude).

CRY MINISTER:
Shock level 3. Cartoon blood in flat red. The man is hidden under the pack and we see meat flung up, never open
wounds. No real logos or real account names (parody handle @TheTikTokPM). No hanging pub sign, which can read as
rude. Graffiti is hand-drawn tags, never font-like.

## 3. THE WORLD (draw once; every shot is a camera looking at it)

FOR ANY FILM: describe the place as one plan from a fixed viewpoint, not shot by shot.
- Layout, left to right, and what stands directly behind the main character.
- Where the ground meets the bottom of every building (no floating, no empty strip).
- What is behind the roofs on each side.
- The scale of people against doors and windows, for near and far lanes.
- Clutter and small details, and where the set runs out at the edges.
- Anything that scrolls (a chase): each building keeps its own look.

CRY MINISTER:
One British high street, left to right: Cash 4 Gold, The Goose and Cranberry (Tudor pub, no hanging sign), ZapBets
(directly behind Andy; yellow poster in both windows reading "FREE £10 BET for new customers*", small print
"*18+. Must not be currently being eaten."), a boarded-up shop with TO LET, a faded sign and plenty of hand-drawn
graffiti tags, Nails & Vapes. A drunk lies face down in a puddle outside the boarded-up shop. Pavement runs right
down to the feet of the shopfronts. Behind the pub a charming church spire; on the right a drab office block. Terraces
along the right are shopfronts with fascia boards, doors, sash windows and drainpipes, not blank walls. Wide shots are
drawn to the frame edge with paving lines all the way down. Adult shoppers are Andy's height or a little shorter;
the nearer lane is drawn larger than the far lane, never child-sized. In the chase the street scrolls steadily and
each plot keeps its own look.
Alleyway (shot 5): a one-point view down a narrow alley with a council WOLF NET hung across it.

## 4. THE CAST

FOR ANY FILM: one block per character: what they look like, their height relative to the main character, clothes,
signature prop, how they walk, and how they behave. Say "reference photo attached" where something is real.
Say which characters must not look like each other. Give the main character an emotion level for each shot.

CRY MINISTER:
- **Andy Burnham:** the Hope Again model. Emotion scale 1-5 for stress. Shots 1-12 level 2 (sincere, earnest);
  shots 13-14 level 4, haunted, red-rimmed eyes (red eyes appear only here); shot 15 despairing; shot 20 angry,
  terrified, panicked, no red eyes. One mouth shape at a time, never gritted teeth drawn over a talking mouth.
- **The man (victim):** a full character, not a blob. Man City shirt (Sam's photo), black tracksuit bottoms, white
  trainers, short light brown hair. He walks up the street unaware, then is taken by the pack.
- **The old lady:** little and petite, pink black-trimmed frock, curly white hair, twitching right eye, far-away
  expression, walking stick, large white-and-pink striped glasses, maroon handbag.
- **Shoppers:** drawn in the show's usual people style, solid (not thin), walking in three-quarter view when
  crossing, entering from off screen, never popping in. They glance at the wolves or at Andy now and then. One man in
  blue stops dead in shot 2 with hands on hips, gives the pack a hard frown, shakes his head three slow big times,
  then walks on.
- **Wolves:** large dire wolves, fast and agile, hungry manic yellow eyes, oversized serrated teeth; they lope and
  bound, fling themselves at the throat, and when feeding rip hunks of meat skywards and cover themselves in blood.
  Cast sheet views: side-on running, leaping, feeding, front snarl (dark throat), tangled in net, and the shrunk
  60% wolf. Side-on open jaws are see-through (no red fill). The same five wolves in the attack and the chase,
  some full size and some smaller in the chase.

## 5. THE SCRIPT, SHOT BY SHOT

FOR ANY FILM: for every shot write the same six lines. Hard cuts unless stated.
- CAMERA: size (close-up, mid, wide) and angle.
- ON SCREEN: who and what is visible, left to right.
- ACTION: beats in order, with approximate times for anything faster than a gesture.
- LOOKS: who looks at whom or what, on each beat.
- STATE: anything that changes and stays changed, and what earlier changes are visible.
- WORDS: the exact line.

Write fixes into the shot, not after it: say which side things enter from, where they land, where props end up,
and which way elbows or arms point.

CRY MINISTER:

**Shot 1.** CAMERA: mid shot, Andy centre, handheld drift. ON SCREEN: Andy, ZapBets behind. ACTION: arms open out
and slightly raised, elbows down, a presenting gesture, not elbows up. LOOKS: into the lens. STATE: street clean.
WORDS: "Now here's a familiar sight you must recognise."

**Shot 2.** CAMERA: same mid shot but Andy fills the right half only; the left half shows the street in front of
the pub. ON SCREEN: Andy right; left, the man, the five wolves, shoppers in the back. ACTION: the pack enters from
the right of the left half in a sweeping arc, launches onto the man, hides him; meat flies up; blood pool forms on
the pavement. A man in blue at the back stops and shakes his head (see cast). Andy gestures and continues without
a pause. LOOKS: Andy to the lens; the shoppers glance at the wolves. STATE: the blood pool and the feeding wolves
stay for every later shot of this spot. WORDS: "Wolf attacks on British high streets. They're violent, they're
concerning, and they've got to stop."

**Shot 3.** CAMERA: mid shot as shot 1. ACTION: gentle gesturing, open palms. STATE: nothing behind him moves except
the feeding. WORDS: "My Labour government are doing everything in their power to get this under control."

**Shot 4.** CAMERA: tight on one large wolf snarling, side-on. ACTION: instant pop to 60% size on "smaller", no
shrink animation. STATE: the smaller wolf stays small. WORDS: "We've implemented smaller wolves."

**Shot 5.** CAMERA: one-point view down the alley. ACTION: the man runs at us, arms swinging naturally (not crossed on
his chest), ducks under the net and veers out of frame; the wolf leaps after him; the net falls on the wolf and its
hem stops at the ground. LOOKS: the man glances back once. STATE: the net stays sagged round the wolf. WORDS: "More
wolf nets down every alleyway."

**Shot 6.** CAMERA: mid two-shot, Andy left, the old lady right. ACTION: Andy thumbs at her with a sly, satisfied
look. On "wolfproof" Kevlar appears on her torso and both arms; she extends her armoured arm; a wolf stands on the
pavement and gnaws on it without success. She does not mind. Shoppers enter from off screen right. LOOKS: Andy to the
lens, then at her; the old lady into the middle distance. WORDS: "And means-tested wolfproof armour for pensioners."

**Shot 7.** Close-up, sincere and concerned. "But there's more to do."
**Shot 8.** Mid shot, pleading, hands together. "So I'm asking you, begging you."
**Shot 9.** Close-up. "Please get a job."
**Shot 10.** Mid shot, arms wide, imploring. "Any job."
**Shot 11.** A little further away. "We have NO money."

**Shot 12.** CAMERA: comically wide, zoomed in so the action sits just above his name and description (the spire tip
just off the top), paving drawn to the bottom. ON SCREEN: Andy small in the foreground, the whole street behind, the
wolves still feasting on the man, the drunk in the puddle, shoppers (proportioned, walking in from off screen).
WORDS: "You want change when I can barely afford to faff about with the margins."

**Shot 13.** Extreme close-up on his eyes, red-rimmed. Stress 4, haunted. "I'm so stressed."
**Shot 14.** Mid shot. Real desperation, slightly crazed round the eyes, stress 4. "I haven't slept for weeks."
**Shot 15.** Close-up, a face-palm of exasperation, deeply despairing. "I CAN'T stop every wolf attack."

**Shot 16.** CAMERA: mid shot. ACTION: football "COME ON, SON" movement with both fists clenched at chest height,
kept clear of the app's username area. WORDS: "I know it's a problem,"

**Shot 17.** Close-up on the wolves mauling the man, same blood pool and meat as shot 2. "Okay,"
**Shot 18.** Desperate mid shot. "Just STOP YELLING."

(Line 19, "I'm going to keep fighting for a fairer and safer country", was cut because it slowed the joke. A brief
should say which lines may be dropped and who decides.)

**Shot 20.** CAMERA: selfie view filming over his own shoulder; Andy on the right with a bit of shoulder. ACTION: he
runs; the street behind scrolls steadily with the five wolves bounding up it, gaining; his selfie arm rises out of
the frame on the left, reaching towards the lens, above the name and description; on "here come some now" he glances
back over his shoulder at the wolves and back to us; after the line, one held terrified grimace with the mouth
closed. The lead wolf leaps at the lens in the last half second. Hard cut to black. WORDS: "Aw fook, here come some
now."

## 6. CONTINUITY CHECKLIST (ticked off before the first animatic)

FOR ANY FILM: list everything that changes and must stay changed, and what appears in every frame.

CRY MINISTER:
- The man stays eaten in every later wide shot; shot 2 wolves still feed in shots 12 and 17.
- The net stays on the wolf in shot 5; the armour stays on the old lady in shot 6.
- The made-up TikTok name and description sit in every frame and stay clear of faces and fists.
- Shot 4's smaller wolf stays small.
- The landscape is otherwise still, except the wolves, the man, the shoppers and the alley chase.

## 7. VOICE, CAPTIONS AND SOUND

FOR ANY FILM: say which lines Sam records, which recording holds which lines, where his pauses are, which lines run
together as one take, the loudness target, and the caption wording rules. List the sounds from the script and where
they come from.

CRY MINISTER:
Sam records every line, one take each, indoors. Lines 4-6 are one file (cut at Sam's pauses); lines 16-18 are one
file and stay unbroken. Loudness: -16.8 LUFS, with at most 3 dB of soft limiting. Line endings keep their real
consonant and fade smoothly. Captions copy Andy's own words, white, at most 3 words or 18 letters per chunk. Sounds
(from real recordings): wolves biting and chewing, paws chasing footsteps. No made-up sounds.

## 8. THE SCREEN FRAME

FOR ANY FILM: which parts of the platform screen you draw and which are left to the real app; whether the title is
in the video or on the cover.

CRY MINISTER:
Only the account name and description are drawn, ending left of the real app's buttons. The title CRY MINISTER goes
on the cover only, not in the video.

## 9. BEFORE ANYTHING IS SHOWN

FOR ANY FILM: list the questions for Sam (all in one batch, each with a recommended answer), the checks to run, and
the order of what Sam sees.

CRY MINISTER:
Questions: recordings, reference photos, gore level, wolf count (same five in the chase), shot 4 instant pop.
Checks: collision, pop-in, size-scale and script audits; mouths follow the voice. Order: cast sheet, then the set,
then one still per shot, then the animatic.
