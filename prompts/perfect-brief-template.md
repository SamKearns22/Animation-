# The perfect brief (built from the 48 notes on Cry Minister)

Fill in every heading before the first picture. Each heading exists because leaving it out cost Sam a round on
Cry Minister (the note numbers are in brackets). Things already fixed in CLAUDE.md and the guides (TikTok size, the
sign-off, circle hands, smooth movement, no code-made sound effects, animatic first, asking before renders) are
deliberately not repeated here.

## 1. THE JOKE
- One sentence: the joke in plain words.
- The turn: the moment it changes from normal to absurd (shot number).
- What the main character never does or notices (for example "never reacts to the wolves until the last line").
- Shock level (1-5) and what is allowed on screen (cartoon blood in flat red, no open wounds, and so on).

## 2. THE WORLD (draw it once, then every shot is a camera looking at it)
- Layout, left to right, as a list. Say what is directly behind the main character and which parts are only
  glimpsed. (notes 3, 31)
- Ground level: where the pavement, kerb and road meet the feet of every building, so nothing floats and no empty
  strip sits under the action. (note 2)
- Sky and skyline: what is behind the roofs on each side (a landmark one side, something dull on the other). (note 4)
- Size scale: each person's height against a door, a shopfront and the other people, in each lane (near, far).
  (notes 16, 21)
- Texture and clutter wanted: graffiti, boarded windows, litter, posters. Say "hand-drawn, not font-like".
  (notes 5, 16)
- What must not appear: anything that could look rude, brand logos, real names. (note 15)
- Edge of the set: how far the camera may swing or zoom before the set runs out. Wide shots must be drawn to the
  edge of the frame. (note 44)
- Anything that scrolls (a chase down a street): say that every building keeps its own look and the loop never
  jumps. (note 48)

## 3. BACKGROUND LIFE (always asked for afterwards, so ask for it now)
- Passers-by: how many, who they are, which way they walk, how they react to the main event (glance, stop, shake
  head). (notes 7, 24, 33)
- Where they enter and leave: from off screen, never appearing in shot. (note 47)
- One small side-story in the corner of the set (a drunk in a puddle). (note 6)
- Shopfront details that carry a second joke, with the exact wording of any sign or poster. (note 37)

## 4. THE PEOPLE (one block each, plus a "must not look like" line)
- Name, height compared with the main character, build, clothes, hair, one signature prop.
- Reference photo from Sam, or "invent". If a shirt, badge or face is real, say so and attach the picture.
  (note 20)
- Walk style: three-quarter view when moving across the screen, never facing forward while sliding sideways.
  (note 22)
- How solid they must look: no thin walkers, no flat blobs. Anyone who is attacked or in the foreground is a full
  character. (notes 11, 34)
- Emotion scale for the main character: a number or named level per shot (for example "stress 4, haunted" for
  shots 13-14; "angry, terrified, panicked, no red eyes" for the finale). (notes 10, 26)
- Faces: say which mouth shapes and eye states each emotion uses, and that only one mouth shows at a time.
  (notes 17, 35, 45)

## 5. THE CREATURES OR VEHICLES (anything new that moves in an unusual way)
- Every view needed on the cast sheet: side-on running, leaping, feeding, front snarl, tangled, shrunk to scale.
  (note 1)
- Anatomy rules: open jaws see-through side-on, dark throat from the front.
- How many, and are they the same ones in every shot? Which are full size and which smaller.
  (note 26)
- How they enter the action: from which side, in what arc, and where they land, so none overshoots. (note 23)

## 6. THE SHOTS (a numbered table; one line per shot)
| # | camera (size and angle) | what is on screen | who looks at whom | action beats with times | words |
- Hard cuts only unless said.
- For shots where something pops, shrinks or appears: "instant" or "gradual". (shot 4)
- For props appearing on a person (armour): which limbs it covers and what interacts with it. (notes 9, 14)
- For any prop that moves (net, sign, ball): where it ends up and that it stops at the ground. (notes 13, 25)
- For any limb that reaches the camera (selfie arm): where the arm starts, where it leaves the frame, and what
  the person looks at on each beat. (notes 29, 30, 36, 41)
- For any pose from a real-world gesture (football "COME ON", presenting hands): describe the pose and which way
  the elbows point. (notes 43, 46)
- For shots with two events at once (speech and action): which one the camera favours and what the speaker does.

## 7. KEEP TRUE (continuity list, ticked off before the first animatic)
- What changes and then stays changed (blood, the eaten man, the net on the wolf). (note 25)
- Which later shots must show the earlier action still going on.
- What is on screen in every shot (the full app screen, the handle, the caption position).
- What is measured from a real phone screen and must stay clear of faces and fists. (notes 8, 42, 43)

## 8. VOICE AND CAPTIONS
- Exact lines, numbered; which recording holds which lines; where the pauses are. (notes 32, 38, 39)
- Lines run together in one take stay one piece.
- Cut-lines: say which lines may be dropped if the film is slow, and who decides. (note 40)
- Loudness target for the finished film and a ceiling on how much squashing is allowed. (note 32)
- Caption chunk size and wording rules (copy the speaker's own words, at most three words per chunk).

## 9. SOUND (only what the script names)
- Sounds from real recordings that Sam supplies, listed by shot. Nothing made in code. (note 18)

## 10. THE SCREEN FRAME (platform overlay and cover)
- Which on-screen parts to draw (name, description only) and which to leave to the real app. (note 42)
- Whether a title is in the video or only on the cover. (note 28)

## 11. BEFORE ANYTHING IS SHOWN
- Run the collision, pop-in, size-scale and script audits.
- Show: cast sheet, then the set from above, then one still per shot, then the animatic.
- Questions for Sam: ask all of them in one batch, each with a recommended answer.
