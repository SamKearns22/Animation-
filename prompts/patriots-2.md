## The joke
The protester from The Patriots writes the RNLI a letter: he respects them, but some of the "people" they
rescue are people he personally doesn't like, so they are traitors. Then he has a heart attack, and the NHS
treats him exactly the way he wanted the RNLI to treat others: the doctor "just doesn't like him personally"
and everyone goes for lunch.
- The target is his double standard: he wants lifesavers to pick and choose, and expects to be saved himself.
- The doctor's line deliberately echoes his own words ("people I personally don't like" / "I just don't like
  him personally"). Keep that echo clear in the timing and the captions.
- Play it completely straight. Nobody winks. The medical staff are calm, professional and pleasant as they
  walk away.
- **What would ruin it:** mocking the people in the dinghy, or making the heart attack gory or frightening.
  The people in the boat are never the joke.

## The format
- **TikTok, native 1080 x 1920.** Everything that matters stays inside the safe area (x 60-900, y 310-1500).
- **Length:** the script as written runs about 45 seconds. Aim for 35-40 (see the questions).
- **The opening:**
  - no held title card;
  - the first frame already moves (the slow push-in has begun), with typing sound from the start;
  - **his first line starts within about half a second**;
  - the title **THE PATRIOTS 2** in our standard treatment, gone by about 1 second, over the dark room and
    never over his face.
- **No single shot longer than about 6-8 seconds** (the lesson from Mossad). Long speeches get a cut to an
  insert or a reaction.
- **The ending:** a hard cut to black after his unhappy gurgle, with picture and sound cutting together
  (a few-millisecond fade so it doesn't click), then nothing.

## The look

  deadpan staging, hard cuts.
- **The protester is the same man as in The Patriots:** reuse him from `source/peepee.py` (`protester()`):

- Everyone else is fictional: no real person and no caricature of anyone real.
- **Real organisations are suggested through colour and generic signs, never their logos:**

  - the ambulance by green and yellow checks and the word AMBULANCE;
  - the hospital by a generic "A&E" sign.
  - "RNLI" is spoken and captioned, but no RNLI or NHS logo appears on screen.
- **Everyone has legs and feet,** and every background person has a clear action.
- **Props at real size.** No real brands: plain lager cans, plain takeaway tubs.

## The places and shots
Timings are rough until the recordings arrive.

1. **His room, at night (about 0-9 s).**
   - A dark box room. The only light is the computer screen: a cool blue glow on his balaclava, hands and
     the desk, with everything else in near-black blue shadow.
   - On the desk:
     - empty curry takeaway tubs (foil and plastic, lids askew);
     - a few crushed and upright cans of lager;
     - the keyboard and mouse.
   - **The camera:** a slow push-in from the upper left of the room towards his face, moving from the first
     frame.
   - He types, and we hear what he types in his own voice. The keyboard clicks follow the rhythm of his
     words.
   - **Patriot:** *"Dear RNLI. As a British Patriot, I have nothing but the deepest respect for the work you
     do saving lives at sea. However, I am writing to register some recent concerns I have had."*
   - To keep the shot under about 8 seconds, cut once to an insert of his fingers jabbing the keys beside a
     lager can (see the questions).
2. **The lifeboat (about 9-14 s).**
   - The lifeboat skims the rough, grey English Channel under a low sky, spray off the bow.
   - The volunteer crew stare ahead, concerned and serious.
   - **Patriot (voice over):** *"I recognise the courage and resilience your people require to do what they
     do."*
3. **The dinghy (about 14-21 s).**
   - The lifeboat draws alongside an overloaded grey inflatable: about a dozen people, mostly working-age
     men, Iraqi, Somali, Moroccan and other Middle Eastern and African people.
   - Draw them with dignity and individual faces: no caricature, orange life jackets on most, soaked, cold
     and tired.
   - A crew member reaches out a hand.
   - **Patriot (voice over):** *"What you've failed to consider is that some of the so-called 'people' you go
     to rescue are those who I personally don't like."*
4. **His room again, from the screen's side (about 21-28 s).**
   - The camera sits where the monitor is, looking straight at his lit face, and keeps pushing in slowly,
     closer now.
   - **Patriot:** *"The lot of you are, on reflection, traitors. Consider this the start of a lengthy
     campaign of harassment in which I shall-"*
   - **Then, in the same shot:** his eyes cross; a strangled sound; he clutches his chest and topples
     sideways off his chair, out of frame.
   - A can rolls off the desk.
5. **The ambulance (about 28-31 s).**
   - Inside the ambulance. He lies on the stretcher, balaclava still on, with an oxygen mask over the mouth
     hole and monitor leads attached.
   - A male and a female paramedic tend to him calmly. She has the line; he rolls his eyes in agreement.
   - **Female paramedic:** *"He's treating us like a bloody taxi service."*
6. **A&E (about 31-42 s).**
   - He is wheeled through the doors of A&E on a trolley, with medical staff around him.
   - They gather round the bed. One is a white British doctor: forties, calm, kind-looking.
   - **Female paramedic:** *"We believe it's a heart attack, Doctor."*
   - The doctor nods.
   - **Doctor:** *"This is an urgent situation. This man's life is in serious danger. Unfortunately, I just
     don't like him personally. Shall we go get lunch?"*
   - Cut to a reaction on "I just don't like him personally": a close-up of the protester's eyes going wide
     over the mask.
   - Back on the doctor, everyone nods agreeably and they all walk off together, chatting.
7. **The ending (about 42-44 s).**
   - The protester alone on the trolley in the empty bay; the monitor beeps steadily.
   - He lets out an unhappy gurgle.
   - **Hard cut to black.**

    - keyboard clicks in step with his words;
    - a can clink as his hand knocks it;

    - the gurgle;

## Captions
- Our standard captions: bold white with a black outline, centred on the frame, wrapped to at most 700 pixels in
  balanced rows, inside the safe area. Keep the rows short (aim for under about 42 characters). Split the long
  letter into readable chunks, timed to his voice.
- Write every word plainly, exactly as spoken. Mossad showed that plain captions on a sensitive topic do not
  stop distribution.
- 'people' keeps its quotation marks (he types them).
- Non-speech sounds (the strangled noise, the gurgle) are not captioned.

## Things to handle carefully
- **The people in the dinghy are real people in danger,** shown with dignity and never as a joke or a threat.
  The laugh is only ever at the protester.
- **The heart attack** is comic in its timing (the letter cut off mid-word), never in its detail:
  - no gore;
  - no death: he is alive and gurgling at the end;
  - nothing frightening.
- **The RNLI and NHS staff** are shown as competent and calm. The doctor's line is the protester's own logic
  turned back on him, not a comment on real doctors.
- **No real brands or logos** (lifeboat, ambulance, hospital, cans, takeaway).
