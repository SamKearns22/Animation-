## 1. The shot as written

Screenplay (Sam, 19 Nov): "Follow Jason as he leans out of the window (camera moves left with him until we hang out
with him); streets whip by: shops, people. He fires several shots back. The leading police car is hit: the windscreen
shatters in front of the driver, it goes out of control, smashes through a fire hydrant and bursts into a shop front
in a ball of flame. Follow him back in, same position and look as before."

Shot 3 comes straight before it and flows into it (Sam, chat 13: "Scene 3 flows into scene 4"): the windscreen
three-shot, camera on the bonnet looking in. Jason front passenger (frame left), Lucia driving (frame right), the
autistic woman centre back. Light: the opening's pink sunset. Plan length 6.2 s (film time 10.6-16.8 s, before the
opening grew by 3 s).

Sam's other standing notes that touch it: the crash is NOT softened ("GTA is a violent satire and there's no reason to
pretend otherwise"); only game references for Jason, Lucia, cars, streets; a Vice City backdrop at the end of the
street and white cloud streaks; the people on the pavements must act (walk, watch, duck, run) and stand on the ground.

The beat list (chat 11's walk-through, Sam: "That was a very good summary"; the build's checklist since):
1. 0-0.5 s: start on shot 3's exact frame (same position and lens).
2. 0.5-1.7 s: camera swings left round the car's front corner to Jason's window while he rises and turns; the window
   frame cuts him at the right place.
3. 1.7-3.0 s: we hang outside with him: Jason to the hips through an OPEN window, arm back over the roof, pistol
   readable, face along the arm; he, his arm and the lead cruiser line up.
4. Shots at 1.95, 2.30, 2.62, 2.92 s: flash, recoil in arm and shoulder, a small frame kick; shot 1 sparks off the
   cruiser's bonnet, 2-4 craze its windscreen more each time; the driver flinches.
5. 3.0-4.55 s: pan with the slewing cruiser over the kerb, through the hydrant (water jet), into a shop front (fireball,
   black smoke); people react and scatter; crash big enough to enjoy with Jason still in frame.
6. 4.55-6.2 s: back round to shot 3's exact frame; Jason settles into his seated pose (last frame = first frame).

## 6. Checks and tools built because of shot 4

- Rule 17 rewritten (Sam, 20 Nov: "'Anything against anything' should be the rule across all clipping checks"):
  anything nearer the camera hides anything behind it, every listed thing, every frame (the depth-order check).
- The body check (chat 12): a body drawn through a solid (door, window pillar, roof, floor), a body part rubbed out
  though it is in front, feet off or below the pavement, and a person drawn at the wrong height for their distance.
  It fails version 3 on exactly Sam's faults.
- One kit for everything (chat 13): the street and cars now draw with the people kit's sharpness setting.
- See-through glass on the check's pictures (chat 15), so cars seen through windows stop raising false faults.

## 8. Lessons for future films (draft; nothing here is a rule until Sam says so)

1. Judge a clip at full size, beat by beat, against the screenplay's words, before sending. A thin strip of small
   frames hides everything that matters.
2. A clean check means only what that check looks at. Say what it cannot see.
3. When a fix changes the camera, compare it side by side with the version before; a fix can make a shot worse.
4. Build the shot before it first when shots flow into each other (shot 3 before shot 4): the first frame is inherited.
5. Every part of the scene draws with the same kit settings; a size mismatch shows up as people sinking, cars
   stretching and people on the grille.
6. Real-size cars and cartoon-size heads clash: check head room at the first frame of every car shot.
7. A body seen from behind needs its own drawing; a front view turned round reads as a lump.
8. Background people are part of the action: walking, reacting to every event, feet on the ground every frame.
9. Ambitious camera moves need the open space to move the actor (Sam: "Give Jason an open window").
