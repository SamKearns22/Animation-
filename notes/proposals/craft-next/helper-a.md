## The real decision
Not "which framing lesson goes into rule 10", but: how every shot gets a frame Sam agreed before building, and how the build is held to it. One lever across 36 notes.

## Root cause
The frame is never a measured, binding spec. Rule 11 asks for a pencil sketch of 3-4 key shots, sent once; every other shot's frame is whatever the tools produce from the plan, and nothing compares the built frame to what was agreed. So size, density and placement drift, and Sam first meets the real frame at the animatic. EPR's pencil sheet was not at real size or camera, and nothing checked the build against it.

## Approaches weighed
1. Framing lessons into rule 10 with a still check on built frames: about 13 notes, caught after building.
2. Reframe: rule 11's sheet becomes a layout card for every shot, drawn from the plan in metres through the real camera with people as labelled real-size blocks; Sam's Y; then the built frame is checked against its card.
3. Learn first: re-tag the 36 notes by "seen on still or moving" and "had an agreed frame", from the animatic records.
4. Contact and eyeline check (11 notes).
5. A composition critique of the first animatic: catches only after the build is paid for.
Chosen: 3, then 2.

## Proposal
**Step 1, learn (changes nothing):** one Sonnet chat re-tags the 36 notes with two columns (seen on still / moving; agreed frame yes / no), About 0.3% of a week. If more than a third are movement-only, the cards cannot reach the number alone and Sam is told so.
**Step 2, one change (its own Y):** rule 11 merged, not a new rule. Every shot gets a layout card: real camera, the plan's people as named blocks at true size, its focus and why, camera height and distance and why. One sheet before building. Enforced by pre-flight, which refuses the cast stage while a shot in the film's shot list has no card; and by a match check on each animatic that compares each shot's built frame to its card (focus person's height within 20%, centre within a tenth of the frame, inside the safe area, clear of the app buttons) and prints the "Checks:" line.
Number to beat: at most 5 frame-related notes per film (from 10-13) on the next two films, none repeated; measured by tagging Sam's notes with the census tags at the end-of-film review.
Cost: about 1.5% once to build (extends the set sketch, rough sheet, pre-flight), about 0.5-1% per film after, inside 15%.
Sam would notice: a sheet of labelled frames for every shot instead of 3-4 sketches; fewer "too far / too thin" notes.
