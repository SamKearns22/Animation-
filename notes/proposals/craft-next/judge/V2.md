## The real decision
Not "which census row next" but: how does a shot's design (who is where, how big, from what height, what fills the tall frame) get fixed once and then held from plan to finished frame across every chat of a film. The three census rows are one lever; splitting them into three yeses would make three paper checks.

## Root cause
Shot design has no record that a check can read. The composition sheet covers 3-4 key shots, says nothing measurable (lead's size in frame, camera height, where the focus sits), and any sheet satisfies the gate. Later chats build from code, not from an agreed frame, so nothing notices when the built frame no longer matches the intent (Blessed 7: gatehouse at 18 m, "Brand a speck"; Blessed 32; Remakes 32 "men behind KG"). Rule 10 says "planned up front" but relies on a chat reading it.

## Approaches weighed
1. **Rule text:** F1, F2, F10 into rule 10. Cheap, but a chat can comply on paper; nothing compares plan to frame.
2. **Reframe: a shot card per shot, measured against the built frame.** Each shot's card states camera height and why, the lead's height on screen, which band each layer sits in, the focus point. Pre-flight measures the rendered key frames (person boxes already exist for the critique's close-ups, rule 89) and refuses a shot that drifts from its card or leaves the focus outside the safe area or half the frame empty.
3. **A blind framing judge:** a fresh helper looks at every shot at phone size against the brief. Catches taste, but repeats rule 89's critique, which already passes these frames.
4. **Learn-first:** sort the 32 notes into plan fault vs build fault from the record, then build only the half that matters.
5. A moving rough: declined 16 Nov; not re-proposed.

Chosen: 4, then 2 sized by its result.

## Proposal
**Step 1, learn (changes nothing, about 0.5% of a week).** One Sonnet chat reads the 32 notes and, for each, what the plan, code or sheet said for that shot before the note: plan fault, build fault, or Sam's late choice. Output: three counts and the shots. The record files already hold this (notes tables, "Why:" columns, plan files).

**Step 2, one change, its own Y.** If build faults are a third or more: the shot card plus the frame measurement (approach 2), enforced by pre-flight refusing an animatic whose cards are missing or whose key frames miss them; a miss is fixed or named on the "Checks:" line. If plan faults dominate: the shot card is filled for every shot (not 3-4) and sent as the rule 11 sheet drawn from the cards, and the gate refuses a sheet built after building began or with no people placed. Either way rule 10 gains F1, F2, F10 as the card's fields. About 1-1.5% of a week once; about 0.1-0.2% per film to fill cards; set against 15% per film, each avoided staging round costs far more (Blessed's round 2 ran several chats).

**Number to beat:** shot-design notes (the census's three rows, same tags) at most 3 per film on the next two films (baseline 8-13), none returned twice. Measured at each film's end-of-film review by the same tagging.

**Sam would notice:** wides where the lead is the right size and the frame is full the first time; one sheet of every shot's plan, if step 2 goes that way.
