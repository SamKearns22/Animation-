Rules that prove themselves

Report on lesson 7, "no rule without a working check" (your option 1). Chain A, chats 1 to 3, 22–23 Nov. Your ask: "report back on all changes to rules, what difference you have made, and how it should behave in the future."

The short version

Before: a rule counted as soon as it was written. Nobody knew whether its check had ever caught anything. Now a rule only counts when a test has shown its check catching a real past mistake (one of your notes, a still you marked, a message). A rule whose check stops working shows up as broken at the start of the next chat. A new rule written without a past mistake to prove it is refused at the handover.

42 → 37rules proven on a past mistake. The new count is lower because every proof was re-checked strictly; 9 more are partly proven
4 → 10of the 38 notes you gave that a rule should have stopped, now caught by a test on that exact mistake
26rules that can't have a working check yet (questions below)

The real measure is fewer notes from you. That can't be measured until the next film ends. Step 1's baseline: 38 of your 65 notes on Milburn and Grand Theft Autism were faults a rule should have stopped.

How the rules work now

One list of every rule, each with its proof new

Before
The rulebook marked 12 rules "by hand" and 24 with no check at all. Nothing showed which checks had ever caught a mistake. Step 1 counted: 61 of 103 never had.
Now
All 103 rules are in one list. Each is marked proven, partly proven, judged by the fresh-eye judge, next to build, waiting on another build, or can't yet. "By hand" is no longer allowed as a status.
Difference
Every chat now opens with one line: how many rules are proven, and the next three worst. This morning that line caught four proofs marked broken (captions, the 1.1× speed-up, the cost audit). I re-ran all four and they pass.
From now on
When a proof stops catching its mistake, the next chat sees it as broken and fixes it first.

No new rule without its proof new

Before
A rule could be written and never tested.
Now
A new rule must come with a saved past mistake and a test that catches it, or the handover is refused. This applies to new rulebook rows and new rules in my instructions file alike.
Difference
Not measured yet. No new rule has been added since this went in. Your answer to question 1 below will be the first real test.
From now on
Each rule you approve arrives with its proof in the same save.

Changes put to you can't slip through changed

Before

Now
A let-through blocks the handover and every animatic and final until it has been routed properly or shown to be a false alarm. Writing "noted" no longer clears it.

It caught one straight away: an out-of-date judgement on a staging picture in the other lesson chain, which that chain has to settle before its next animatic. The weekly count will be measured at the next film's end.
