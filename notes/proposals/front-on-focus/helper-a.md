## The real decision
The decision covers every future person in every chat, not four Milburn people: how a person shows where they are looking at front-on quality, and how every stage reliably reaches its best tool. The bigger lever is the guides and checks that let a chat pick turning, or an older route, without noticing.

## Root cause
1. Turning was the only way to show direction. Turns snap to 45 degrees, and front heads have no uneven brows, no off-centre mouth and no slide. So Sam's "focus to the other side" became a turn.
2. On films with a rooms plan, the check reads the plan, not the drawing.
3. The 18 Nov fix counted parts kept, not whether a turned person matches the front one.
4. The guides list tools by who someone is, and point real people to the older head.

## Approaches weighed
1. Make turned views as good as front: this failed twice (face chats 6-12, then 18 Nov) and goes against rule 38.
2. Reframe as staging: square bodies to the camera and use today's eyes, tilt and gestures. Free, but the read on Andy is weak.
3. A focus setting on every front head, plus a drawn-angle check on every film.
4. Reframe the tools question: one head for everyone, so there is no weaker head to pick.
5. Learn first: a census of drawn angles and of best tools before anything is retired.

Chosen: 5, then 3, then fix the guides; 4 waits for evidence.

## Proposal
**Learn** (changes nothing, about 0.8% of a week):
- (a) Seconds each film drew people past 25 degrees, and the notes those shots got.
- (b) For each stage, which tools the last three films used, and which Sam's notes favoured or faulted. A tool with no use in three films gets a "Lost:" line; nothing is deleted.
- (c) One invented person drawn front on, on both heads, on one sheet.

**Change 1** (its own yes, about 0.3%): pre-flight reads the drawn angle on every film and refuses a person past 25 degrees without a listed reason. Beat: 11.9 s down to 0.

**Change 2** (its own yes, about 2%): a `focus` setting from left to right on every front head. It moves the pupils; slides the eyes, nose, mouth and glasses as whole pieces; sets the brows uneven; offsets the mouth; tilts the head. It also becomes a move in the moves kit. A kit test checks that at full focus every part is still drawn and nothing is warped. Beat: Sam passes a sheet of Andy, Kemi and one invented person, then zero turned-quality notes on the next film.

**Change 3** (its own yes, about 0.5%): the catalogue and the people kit's map list the best head first. Pre-flight refuses a person who has a face-tool pick but is drawn on the older measured head. Beat: no film uses a weaker head because the guides pointed to it.

**Change 4** (only if Learn (c) favours the house head, about 3%): invented people drawn on the house head.

**Retirements:** each one is a numbered line from Learn (b) and needs Sam's Y.

Learn plus Changes 1-3 are one-off builds of about 3.6%, about what Milburn's single fix cost. Sam would see people looking where they should, at front quality.
