## The real decision
How does every future person show where they are looking at front-on quality, and how does every stage reliably reach its best route? This is a question about how films are built across all chats, not about four Milburn people. A bigger lever than any new head drawing: the guides and checks that let a chat pick turning, and pick the older route, without noticing.

## Root cause
1. Turning was the only tool for showing direction. The engine snaps every turn to 0 or 45 degrees, and front heads have no uneven brows, no off-centre mouth and no feature slide. So when Sam said "focus to the other side", a chat could only turn the person. Milburn's code comment shows that misreading of his chat-6 words.
2. The check that should catch it reads the plan's facing, not the drawing, whenever a film has a rooms plan.
3. The 18 Nov fix measured the wrong thing. It counted parts kept, not whether a turned person looks as good as the same person front on.
4. Findability: the guides list tools by who someone is (famous or invented), and the one route they point real people to is the older one.

## Approaches weighed
1. Make turned views as good as front ones (better 3D head, necks, glasses). This has failed twice (face chats 6-12, then 18 Nov) and goes against rule 38.
2. Reframe as staging: square bodies to the camera and carry direction with the eyes, tilt and gestures that already exist. Free, but on Andy the read is weak.
3. A focus piece on every front head that moves whole pieces, plus the drawn-angle check on every film.
4. Reframe the tools question as one head for everyone, so no weaker head is left to pick.
5. Learn first: a census of drawn angles and of best routes before anything is retired.

Chosen: 5, then 3, then the guide fix, with 4 waiting on evidence.

## Proposal
**Learn (changes nothing, about 0.8% of a week):**
- (a) From each film's frames: seconds of people drawn past 25 degrees, and the notes tied to them.
- (b) Per stage: the routes each of the last three films used, and the routes Sam's notes favoured or faulted. Each route with zero use in three films is listed with a "Lost:" line. Nothing is deleted.
- (c) One invented person drawn front on, on both heads, on one sheet.

**Change 1 (its own yes, about 0.3%):** rule 38's check reads the drawn angle on every film. Pre-flight refuses anyone drawn past 25 degrees without a THREE_QUARTER_OK reason. Number to beat: 11.9 s of unlisted turned people goes to 0.

**Change 2 (its own yes, about 2%):** a `focus` setting from left to right on every front head (house head and people kit). It moves both pupils, slides the eyes, nose, mouth and glasses as whole pieces, sets one brow up and one down, offsets the mouth, and tilts the head. It also becomes a move in the moves kit. Enforced by a kit test: at full focus every part is still drawn and nothing is warped. Number to beat: Sam passes a sheet of Andy, Kemi and one invented person, and the next film gets zero turned-quality notes.

**Change 3 (its own yes, about 0.5%):** the catalogue's people row and the people kit's MAP put the best head first. Pre-flight refuses a person who has a face-tool pick but is drawn on `head: measured`. Number to beat: no film draws a weaker head that the guides pointed to.

**Change 4 (only if Learn (c) shows the house head is better):** invented people on the house head. Priced separately, about 3%.

**Retirements:** one numbered line each from Learn (b), each needing Sam's Y.

These are one-off builds of about 3.6% in total for Learn and Changes 1-3, roughly the cost of Milburn's one 3/4 fix. Sam would notice people looking where they should at front quality, and a short list of tools to retire.
