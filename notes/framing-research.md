## What it does (as proposed to Sam)
1. **Read** (primary sources, not search summaries): Bruce Block, *The Visual Story* (space, movement, contrast and
   affinity); Marcos Mateu-Mestre, *Framed Ink* 1 and 2; Hans Bacher, *Dream Worlds* (Disney layout); the studio talks
   on the *Mulan* Hun charge, *The Lion King* stampede and *The Prince of Egypt* Exodus; Every Frame a Painting on
   Kurosawa (composing movement) and Vashi Nedomansky on *Mad Max: Fury Road* (centre framing for fast action).
2. **Frame study:** about 16 stills of great crowd, vista and battle or riot shots (*Ran*, *Seven Samurai*,
   *Lawrence of Arabia*, *The Battle of Algiers*, *Children of Men*, *Athena*, *Les Misérables* 2019, *Mad Max: Fury
   Road*, *Mulan*, *The Lion King*, *The Prince of Egypt*, *Akira*), each marked up: eye level, the focus and how the
   eye gets there, value groups, depth layers, movement directions, scale cues; and what survives a 9:16 vertical crop
   (our frame). One labelled study sheet, sent to Sam.
3. **Write-up:** rewrite `research/crowds-and-big-action.md` as numbered lessons (each names the closest rule or kit
   piece, searched first, and what is new), for Sam to pick by number; nothing becomes a rule before his numbers.
4. **Apply:** re-stage Protest Tiers' four scenes with the lessons (camera, eye level, focus per beat, layers), as a
   proposal in that film's notes; the film's pencil roughs come after (Sam: "let's wait and see how it turns out").
