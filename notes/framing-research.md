## Findings (chat 2, 12 Nov): what the sources say
- **Mad Max: Fury Road** (Vashi Nedomansky, vashivisuals.com, read whole): Miller on set, "Put the cross hairs on her nose! Put the cross hairs on the gun!"; almost every shot centre-framed so 2,700 shots need "no hunting of each new shot for the point of interest"; "eye trace": motion in one shot leads the eye to where the next shot's focus sits (arrow flies L to R, cut to target on the right).
- **Children of Men** (Lubezki, theasc.com, read whole): "We were trying not to do a perfect frame, so it doesn't look framed"; 18 mm lens at a distance, no POV, almost no close-ups, "to stay objective"; violence like a documentary, "no coverage", no slow-motion close-ups of the gun; the one-shot car scene hides cuts on a bullet impact.
- **Athena** (Gavras in The Quietus; DP Matias Boucard, milesvstephenson.com): real crowd and fireworks, staging "almost military, very precise to create chaos in front of the camera"; "we only followed one guy, no matter what happened"; Boucard: the longer the lens the less you feel camera movement; shot big-sensor so "the audience can decide where to look" between actor and background; "neutral", not "epic on each shot".
- **Ran** (search summaries of the ASC article; the article itself failed TLS): main battle covered from a 9 m platform on a 55 mm lens, a second camera 500 m away on 100 mm; armies kept to screen direction (Jiro's always enter left to right, Saburo's right to left); mostly long shots, very few close-ups.
- **Bruce Block, The Visual Story** (review and publisher summaries; the book itself is lending-only, not read): contrast raises intensity, affinity lowers it, for space, line, shape, tone, colour, movement and rhythm; four spaces: deep, flat, limited, ambiguous.
- **Prince of Egypt** (Henry LaBounta, vfxblog): CG crowd figures kept tiny on screen; the Red Sea matched to Kathy Altieri's paintings. **Mulan** (Before & After, fragments only; full page rate-limited): Hun riders mixed from parts; painted layout used as a projection map to populate the crowd.
- **Not reached:** Framed Ink and Dream Worlds (only unlawful uploads found; skipped); Kurosawa video transcript (Amara 403); film-grab.com (429). Hans Bacher's blog: his ingredient list is an image only.

## Lesson seeds for chat 3 (from the sheet; each still needs its closest rule or kit piece)
1. **Our wide shot is tall.** A 16:9 vista loses two thirds in 9:16 (sheet 2: 3 riders of 9 survive). Stack the layers top to bottom (sky event / target / mass / foreground), not side by side (sheet 3, 14, 15).
2. **Eye level first, and where heads go:** camera at standing height puts every standing head on one line (8, 12, 13, 14); high camera turns a crowd into a river or knot (5, 6, 7).
3. **One focus, the only one of its kind:** brightest, warmest or the only upright (3 fire, 8 the dark armed figure, 12, 16). Two hot spots only if set far apart and joined (15).
4. **A crowd is one shape with one value** against a plain ground; heads all one way = affinity; the change (ripple, split) then reads at once (6, 7, 2).
5. **Backlight makes the silhouette** (13, 2): fire or sky behind, the line goes black; cool one side, hot the other.
6. **Watchers point the eye:** backs in the foreground all looking one way aim us (9); faces lit only where they matter (10).
7. **Lines lead in:** paths, road markings, radiating streaks to the focus (4, 6, 13).
8. **Emptiness is scale:** small figures, big empty ground; long shadows (1). Few close-ups (Ran, Children of Men).
9. **Chaos needs one clear moving thing and a level camera** (11; Lubezki's objective distance; Boucard's long lens; Gavras: follow one person).
10. **Centre the focus when cutting fast** (16): every shot's focus in the same place, and movement at the cut leads to the next shot's focus (eye trace).
11. **Screen direction is fixed per side** (Ran): police always one side, crowd the other, across all four tiers.
12. **Far crowds are tiny and simple** (Prince of Egypt CG crowd; Mulan mixed parts): merges with C1-C2 of the first pass.
