## Reference photos

shoulder straps, side-on, walking; (2) a dragon-boat or canoe crew carrying the hull at the hip on a portage; (3) a
pantomime horse walking (two people, one body, out of step). The rules below come from how the thing must work.

## How it works (the rules to draw it by)
- **One stiff boat on eight pairs of legs.** The boat is rigid: it goes where the hips take it. Each man stands in his
  own ragged hole cut in the floor; the floor sits just below the shortest man's crotch, so every pelvis is inside the
  boat and only legs come out underneath. Taller men stand a little higher in it.
- **Two rows of four**, as a real small inflatable is sat (Patriots 2: a far row and a near row), staggered so every
  head shows. The near tube hides each man from the belly down; the far tube shows behind them.
- **Legs come out through the holes, never through the tube.** Side-on, a leg straight under the near tube would read
  as a leg through the tube, so the soft floor sags a little below the tubes (an unboarded inflatable's floor does under
  weight) and the legs come out of that sag, each pair through a torn hole whose cut flaps hang down round the thighs.
  The hole is fixed in the floor and wide enough for both thighs through the whole stride.
- **Planted feet stay put** while the boat moves on; the legs bend to take up the boat's height. Nobody's leg is ever
  stretched straight to reach the ground.
- **Out of step on purpose** (a comic shuffle): every man the same short, quick steps (one boat, one speed) but each
  starting at his own moment.
- **The rocking comes from the steps:** each man pushes up a little at the middle of his step; the boat takes the
  best-fit line through the eight pushes, so it bobs and tips nose-up and nose-down a little, unevenly. Side-on only the
  nose-up/down tip and the bob show.
- **Sheepish:** heads bowed, eyes forward, shoulders hunched a little; the near row's hands grip the near tube's top.
- **Draw time:** with every man on the same step length and speed, the whole boat repeats every step cycle: draw each
  man's layers once per drawing in the cycle and slide them on.
